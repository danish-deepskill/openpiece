Economy

status: charted, a sea that trades whether you're watching or not

The world trades without you. Merchants sail real routes with real cargo, ports want what they lack and sell what they've got, and prices come from that, not from a shopkeeper's eternal price list. Your ledger is just one more in a sea full of them.

Every price is information: a high number is the sea telling you where a ship full of profit hasn't arrived.

A living market

The intent: supply and demand per port, moved by ships that actually make the crossing. A storm that sinks the grain convoy is felt in bread prices two islands away.

Your ledger

Cargo runs for honest coin. Smuggling for better coin and worse company. Provisioning as a real cost, crews eat, hulls wear, and the voyage that ignores its books ends on a beach somewhere. And piracy, of course: the economy is also a map of what's worth robbing, a merchant's loss book is a pirate's menu.

The underworld

Every economy has a shadow, and this sea's shadow keeps books too. Contraband, powder, things no harbormaster will stamp, sold by brokers who don't ask questions, at prices that do. Because here's the squeeze: the black market reads your poster. The hotter your name burns (see Bounty), the fewer honest ports will serve you, and the underworld knows exactly what your desperation is worth. Infamy doesn't just close doors; it reprices the ones still open.

And the shadow market runs at two speeds, by design:

Stolen goods launder themselves

Grain, powder, cloth: a barrel is a barrel, and nobody can prove whose it was. Fencing cargo is business.

Stolen treasures never wash clean

A graded blade or a named relic carries its own history, and everyone who knows the piece knows it's hot. Fencing a legend is a story, usually somebody else's, about you.

Where it stands

Charted, hand in hand with Activities (what you make feeds what the world trades). It lands after the seamless sea gives merchants real routes to sail.

Read the prices like a chart, they're telling you where to sail.
