Activities

status: charted, the peaceful half of the pirate's day, planned

A pirate's day isn't all powder and blood. There's a hold to fill, a crew to feed, a hull that won't patch itself, and a whole honest living to be made by people who never fire a shot:

fishing cooking mining woodcutting crafting smithing shipwright
Cast lines to become a fisherman. Work the forge to become a smith. You become what you do, and you can't become everything.

Learn by doing

Skills grow by use, Old School RuneScape-style, and the caps are the point. Being the cook, or the shipwright, is an identity, which is exactly what makes a crew of specialists worth assembling (see Crew). And the quiet summit: the sea keeps official lists of its great blades, and a smith can spend a career forging toward them. A trade skill with a legend at the top.

The doing feeds the voyage

None of it is a side-minigame in a vacuum: fish feeds the crew, the crew's morale holds the watch, the forge outfits the ship, the ship carries the cargo that pays for all of it. Every activity is a gear in the voyage's machine, and each one is a way to rise for players who'd rather build than burn.

Where it stands

Charted, alongside the Economy it feeds. The learn-by-doing law is already settled design; the activities themselves land as the world grows the places to do them.

There's more than one way to become a legend of the sea.
