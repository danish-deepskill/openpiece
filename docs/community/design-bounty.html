Bounty

status: charted — fame with teeth, planned in detail

Fame, with teeth. Do something the world would notice, and — if the world actually noticed — it answers. Your bounty poster is your resume, your warrant, and your legend, all on one sheet of paper.

Witnesses and news

Nothing is known just because it happened. Somebody has to see it — and then the story has to travel, port to port, at the speed of ships. Sink a marine cutter with no survivors, and the sea keeps your secret for a while. Do it in front of a harbor, and your name sails faster than you do. Silence witnesses, and… well. That's a choice with its own weight. And when the news finally arrives, it arrives properly: a paper dropped on the deck by a news bird, updated posters folded inside — the world's happenings delivered to you the way this sea intends.

Heat and the response

As your bounty climbs, so does what comes looking: a bored patrol, then competent officers, then names with their own posters. Heat also cools — lie low where nobody knows your face, and the hunt loses your scent. Fame is a resource you spend and a fire you manage.

Rivals

The best enemy is one who rose while you did. The plan seeds a few ambitious souls per sea — the ones with the drive to climb (see World for the promise that they really do). Cross one early, and the captain you embarrassed for a rowboat may come back years later with a fleet and a grudge.

Where it stands

Charted, in unusual detail — witnesses, news travel, heat, and rivalries are one of the most worked-out plans in the backlog. It arrives after crews, because a legend needs someone to tell it to.

Your poster is your resume. Write it deliberately.
