Simulation

status: built, the deterministic engine runs and is test-proven; the seamless sea is designed

Every game calls itself "deeply simulated." This page is what we actually mean by it, and how you could check us.

The world is arithmetic that never stops, and the pretty part you see is just the arithmetic standing up.

The world runs at resolutions, not on rails

Nothing far away ever pauses; it runs at lower resolution. Near you, every swing and footstep is computed in full. A sea away, a trading ship isn't animated wave by wave: she is a departure, a heading, and an arrival the world has already scheduled, one line of math evaluated only when someone asks. A thousand distant voyages cost about as much as a timetable, because that's what they are. A railway doesn't animate its trains; it runs on a timetable, and wheels and steam exist wherever someone stands on a platform. You are the platform.

The bread counter

Watch a baker's stall holding ten loaves, sail away for a day, come back. You won't see "the same ten" (frozen), and you won't see a refilled template (the theme-park chest). You'll see what the town's ledger honestly says: four loaves, because you returned at dusk after the buying hours, or twenty, because the morning batch just landed. The town traded while you were gone, as cheap arithmetic on its market ledger, and your own fingerprints persist in it: buy three loaves before leaving and everything downstream starts from seven. Leaving town doesn't freeze it or reset it. It just stops the rendering. The arithmetic never left.

Same seed, same world

The whole simulation is deterministic: same starting seed, same decisions, same world, every time, proven by automated replay tests (hundreds of them run on every change). This is the receipt behind every promise on this site. "The world runs without you" isn't a vibe, it's an assertion a test can check: the ship that sailed unwatched arrives at the same port, at the same time, with the same cargo, as the ship that sailed watched. And nothing rerolls when you look twice: revisiting a shop doesn't re-randomize its stock, because observation renders the world, it never rolls it.

Nothing is scripted

There are no quest rails, no boss scripts, no invisible director forcing outcomes. Situations come from systems: the ambush you sailed into was a real crew with a real grudge and a real route (see Story for what that does to storytelling, and Combat for why even the terrifying fights are unscripted). The event director exists, but it aims the world's weather; it never fakes the physics.

Nothing is lost, either

Unloading is never deletion; it's compression, and the world has exactly four tricks:

terrain → a seed changes → deltas lives → ledgers timers → timestamps

The tree you felled and the powder you dropped are small recorded differences. The dropped loot that rots after three days "expires" via one subtraction when anyone next arrives. Four file formats, and the world loses nothing in any of them.

And you're not special to it

The simulation doesn't know which character is yours. Same rules, same growth, same death, same gossip about you as about anyone (see Control). The camera follows you; the world doesn't.

Where it stands

The deterministic engine, the seeded randomness, the fixed timestep, and the replay proofs are built and running today; they've been the foundation since the first commit. The seamless chunk-streamed sea with its full resolution ladder is designed and in final review; the machinery above is how it will breathe. The living content that rides on all this has its own page: World.

A simulated world is a promise. Determinism is the receipt.
