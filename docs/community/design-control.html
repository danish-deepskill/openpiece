Control

status: built, possession and squads today; the command ladder designed; territory charted

You are not the main character, and that's the superpower. Because the world treats you as just another person in it, "you" can be anyone: step out of your pirate and into a fisherman, a marine on watch, a beast in the hills. Control is something a character has, not something you are.

Possess anyone, today

This isn't a planned feature; it's how the engine works right now. Every character runs on the same systems, so handing you the reins of any of them costs the game nothing. It's also the honest foundation multiplayer needs: a second player is just another mind holding another character's reins.

The three altitudes of command

The climb from nobody to Yonko is a climb in scope: the same world, seen from three heights:

individualone body,
footwork and timing
tacticalsquads and fleets,
battle speed
strategicbases and territory,
the speed of days
  • Individual: one body, your hands on everything: the blade, the helm, the gangplank. You win fights by footwork and timing. This is the game today, and it never goes away, every higher altitude is built on people exactly like this one.
  • Tactical: command in the fight, at battle speed. It ladders too: a squad (focus fire, flank, hold the line) → a division of squads (commit or refuse them, feign a retreat, spend one delay to buy another flank time) → a fleet (formations, landing waves, the withdrawal line, the altitude of a war between powers). Squad orders exist in-engine now; the upper rungs are designed.
  • Strategic: the campaign, at the speed of days. Run a base: patrols, alert levels, investigations, bounties posted. Rule a territory: raid targets, alliances, tribute, promotions, wars. At this height you stop seeing the world through eyes and start seeing it through reports: sightings, patrol logs, rumor, which means you can be lied to, and a clever enemy will. Charted as the endgame.

One ladder, one vocabulary

Here's the part that makes it Openpiece: those altitudes aren't a "strategy mode" bolted on. Marine commodores and rival captains climb the same ladder with the same orders: the game's AI commanders literally choose from the menu you'll use. Possess a garrison's commodore, and the garrison is yours: its patrols, its alerts, its bounty board. And the altitudes compose downward: a ruler's "raid that island" becomes a fleet, becomes divisions, becomes squads, becomes one deckhand's very individual morning, and you're allowed to go be that deckhand while your captains handle the rest. And the ladder runs both ways: before anyone sails under your flag, you can sail under someone else's, take orders, earn trust, climb (see Crew).

Orders, not puppets.The people under your flag stay people. Your swordsman will hold the line you drew until holding it means dying for nothing, and then he'll break, because he's a person with his own read of the fight, not a chess piece.

Command is leadership

Command in this game is the skill of getting real, stubborn, self-preserving characters to do what the plan needs, which is to say: it's leadership, with everything annoying and glorious that implies.

A note on multiplayer

The same sea, shared, is the far shore this project steers by, server-authoritative, no client-side lies. It stays possible precisely because no player is special to the simulation. It comes when the single-player world has earned it.

The world doesn't know you're special, so you're free to be anyone in it.
