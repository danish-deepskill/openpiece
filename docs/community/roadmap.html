The manifest

Every feature walks the same ladder, and these badges are the project's REAL internal states — not marketing labels. Built = running in-engine today. The game itself is pre-MVP: nothing is released yet, and that's the point of showing the ladder honestly.

charted → researched → designed → building → built

Sailing 1.0

Built

A real wind field, points of sail per rig, four sail states, a true anchor (the anchor-turn works), a windable + lashable wheel, leeway and sternway — and in irons is a delay, never a prison.

Walkable ships & boarding

Built

Decks are real places: cross the gangplank, leap the rail, ride a moving deck seamlessly. Three hulls with genuinely different handling.

Layered combat core

Built

Devil Fruit + weapon + fighting style through one engine — the Gomu fruit end-to-end, swords, guns, brawling.

A living island

Built

Factions with real relations, self-defending townsfolk, wildlife, hunting squads — and possession of any character.

The honest engine

Built

Deterministic simulation proven by replay tests; everything data-driven; ~480 automated tests. Multiplayer-shaped from the first commit.

The Haki ↔ Logia capstone

Building

Armament Haki countering a Logia's intangibility — emerging from the combat engine's own rules, no special-case code. Closes Island 0.

Ship impacts & cannons

Designed

Collisions, ramming with real momentum, grounding — then broadside cannons and part damage. First cargo of Island 1.

Combat direction: stamina · posture · parry

Designed

The full fight-feel plan: resource discipline, guard-break posture, telegraphs and punishes.

Ship classes & the upgrade law

Designed

Every hull and part a trade-off, never a strictly-better buy — the no-cheap-power contract, on paper and ready.

The navigator's ladder

Designed

Sailing skill as capability bands — a veteran reads wind, currents, and forecasts a rookie simply cannot see.

AI intelligence ladder

Designed

Enemy smarts as data, 1–10: a thug rushes, a captain feints, reroutes, and counters — same engine, different minds.

Time, calendar & day/night

Designed

The clock substrate half the world's systems assume — watches, routines, seasons of the sea.

The seamless sea

Designed

One continuous chunk-streamed ocean with simulation tiers — distant ships live as ledger entries, near ones in full. In final design review.

Crew & shipboard life

Researched

Roles, jobs, watches, morale — a 95-claim research bank across the genre's best is in; the design pass is next. The crew is the mid-game.

Currents & the structured sea

Charted

Sailing 2.0: currents as chartable geography, wind shadows behind islands, funneling straits, squalls.

Ranged & projectiles

Charted

One substrate for muskets, thrown steel, cannonballs, and fruit shots — skill unlocks verbs, not numbers.

Progression & skills

Charted

Stats, Haki growth, learn-by-doing skills, crafting — power priced in time and risk, never menus.

Economy, trade & activities

Charted

Cargo runs, fishing, bounties, provisioning — the peaceful half of a pirate's ledger.

Marines & organizations

Charted

Ranks, authority, duty and promotion as data — an enemy that is an institution, not a spawn table.

Elements & the dominance ladder

Charted

Fire, ice, lightning, rubber — reactive matchups in combat and out in the world.

Dialogue & the social web

Charted

Talking as real play: reputation, favors, recruitment, betrayal — the other half of "peer".

Territory & the endgame

Charted

The strategic layer: holdings, fleets, rivals — the rise from captain to power.

Multiplayer

Charted

Server-authoritative from the architecture up. Pursued when the single-player sim has earned it.
