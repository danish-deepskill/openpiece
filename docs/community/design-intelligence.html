Minds

status: designed — the intelligence ladder is on paper; basic minds run today

Every enemy runs on the same brain. What differs is how much of it they're allowed to use. A dockside thug and a marine captain aren't different code — they're different minds: one rushes you in a straight line, the other keeps his reach, feints, cuts off your escape, and counters the trick that worked on the last three men.

The intelligence ladder

Smarts as a dial from 1 to 10, set per character as data. Low rungs are honest about being dumb — slow to react, easy to bait. High rungs earn their reputation: they read your patterns, reposition, and make you solve a new problem every few seconds. Rank should be a warning label you learn to respect.

Fair minds only

AI reads the world through the same senses you do — vision cones, hearing, the dark of night. No wallhacks, no aimbots, no knowing where you are because the computer says so. If a captain finds you, it's because someone saw you, or he guessed well.

The gym

Behind the scenes, minds are tuned the way fighters are: in a training ring. An offline scenario league pits intelligence tiers against each other until "a 7 beats a 4" is a measured fact, not a hope — so the difficulty you feel is calibrated, not accidental.

Where it stands

Simple minds run in-engine today (states, profiles, squad hunts). The full ladder, the counter-play, and the gym are designed and waiting their turn in the queue.

Outsmarting an enemy should feel like outsmarting someone.
