Combat

status: built, the core runs today; the full fight-feel is designed

Fighting is action, and it's honest: you move, you aim, you strike, you dodge, no dice rolling behind your back, no fight you couldn't have read if you were watching closely. Reach matters. Position matters. And every fighter in the world, you included, fights by exactly the same rules.

In the game today

Real-time melee and abilities with real reach and real openings, swords, guns, brawling, and Devil Fruit techniques through one engine (see Powers for how they compose). Stamina already gates offense: every swing, dodge, and sprint spends it, and it only refills when you ease off, swing wild and you're spent in front of someone who isn't. Reach is honest and bodily: it comes from your frame plus your weapon, so a Fishman's bare fist out-ranges a human's and a blade adds its steel on top. Stances switch mid-fight. And what you see is what hits, the drawn fist is the punch hitbox. Enemies fight back with genuinely different minds, a thug and a captain are not the same opponent (see Intelligence).

There are no bosses in this game. The terrifying people of this sea are just people: same rules, better bands, sharper minds.

There are no bosses

Deliberately. A "boss fight" is a scripted special case, an arena, phases, health-gated plot armor, and this game refuses special cases on principle. No script is needed (see Powers for how far that gap goes). Which changes everything about how you meet them: an Admiral isn't waiting in a room for you, he's somewhere real, doing something real, and the fight happens wherever your bad luck arranges it. You can run from him. You can ambush him. Ten years from now, you can be him. Even the monstrous war-forms that great battles can spawn play by the engine's rules, nothing in this world fights outside them.

The direction: telegraph and punish

The designed fight-feel, next on the slipway, a duel of reads, not just reflexes:

Two bars, two games

Stamina gates offense (in the game now); posture gates defense: block too much, get pressured too long, and your guard breaks open. Souls-style discipline on the attack, Sekiro-style dueling at the guard.

Parry · block · feint

A timed parry turns an attack and spikes the attacker's posture; a held block braces but feeds your own break; a feint sells a swing that never comes. The best feints have no rhythm, because good enemies learn yours (see Intelligence).

Telegraphs that teach

Dangerous attacks announce themselves with a colored cue that names its answer: this one you parry, this one you dodge, this one you get out from under. The cue tells you what it wants; doing it under pressure is the skill.

No stun-locks

Combo strings end by design, a finisher that launches and resets the exchange, so nobody, including you, gets held in place and deleted. Fairness is a rule of the engine, not a courtesy.

And a word for the skeptics: this kind of depth reads better from above. The whole fight is on screen at once, every windup, every flank, every escape route. Top-down isn't the compromise; it's the vantage point.

Further out

  • Ranged, one substrate: muskets, thrown steel, cannonballs, and fruit shots as one system. Charted.
  • Elements: fire, ice, lightning, rubber; matchups that work in combat and out in the world. Charted.

The price of defeat

An honest open question. Today the game uses a simple arcade respawn, a placeholder, not the plan. What losing should really cost (wounds that heal slowly? captains carried off by their crew, Kenshi-style?) is being decided together with the Crew design, because who drags you off the field changes everything.

Skill should read the fight, not the stat sheet.
