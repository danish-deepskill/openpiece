Sailing

status: built — sailing 1.0 runs in the game today

The sea is not a road, and your ship is not a car. Wind blows across the whole ocean with a will of its own, the water shoves back, and getting anywhere is a negotiation between you, your canvas, and the weather. That negotiation — seamanship — is the game.

Wind with a will

The wind is a character. It has moods — it shifts, it gusts, it dies to nothing — and you read it the way sailors always have: off the world, not off a gauge. Streaks on the water, the belly of your sails, the way the bow falls off when you've lost your angle. Run with it and you fly. Fight it and you stall, canvas flogging, while the fight you were fleeing catches up. A rookie finds out about weather when it arrives; a navigator saw it coming an hour ago — and that gap in reading, not in stats, is what sailing skill means here.

Yes, it's the real thing

Under all of it runs a genuine sailing model — not a car reskinned as a boat. The wind your sails feel changes with your own speed and heading, the way it does on real water. Some angles to the wind are fast, some are slow, and one is flat impossible — and the fast ones almost never point straight at where you're going, which is why real sailing is geometry and patience as much as courage. Hulls carry their momentum, slip sideways when the sea insists, and answer the rudder a beat late. If you've ever sailed, you'll recognize this water immediately. If you haven't, you'll absorb a real skill without noticing — the proudest trick this game has is that learning to sail here is just… learning to sail.

A sea with geography

The charted horizon (Sailing 2.0): the water itself becomes terrain. Currents run like rivers through the open sea — chartable, tradable knowledge, a day saved for those who know them. Islands cast wind shadows where your sails go slack in sight of shore. Straits funnel gales; squalls ambush; whirlpools wait where the charts go quiet; and tides run on the world's own clock, so a harbor that welcomed you at noon can be a churn of white water by night. The sea stops being the space between content — it is the content.

Waters that get wilder

The sea is tiered like everything else in this world. The home shallows are honest water; sail outward and the regions harden, all the way to the final stretches where the weather itself fights dirty and only monsters keep the routes. And between the great seas lie the Calm Belts: no wind at all. Ever. Crossing one means oars, nerve, and hoping the enormous things sleeping under that flat, silent water stay asleep. Every border on the map is a decision.

The sailor's hands

All of it lands in your hands, in-engine today: trim the sails to the wind's angle, shorten canvas before the squall hits instead of after, swing your bow around a dropped anchor in a harbor too tight to turn in, feel the hull slip sideways when the sea shoves — and when you get caught dead into the wind, work your way out like a sailor instead of waiting like a passenger. None of it is a cutscene; all of it is craft.

Ships are places

Decks are real, walkable ground. Cross the gangplank, leap the rail, take the wheel, ride a moving deck without the world noticing the seam — and one day, sail hulls you built yourself, plank by chosen plank.

Where it stands

The wind, the points of sail, the anchor work, the drift — Sailing 1.0 — is built and sailing today. Ship combat (broadsides, ramming with real momentum) is designed and next. The structured sea — currents, wind shadows, the wilder waters — is charted as Sailing 2.0, and it's the pass this page is most excited about.

The gap between the rookie and the navigator is the game.
