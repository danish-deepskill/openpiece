World

status: built — a living island today; the seamless sea and the clock designed

The world doesn't wait for you. Squads hunt, wildlife grazes and gores, townsfolk defend their own, factions feud — whether you're there or not. You witness more than you trigger. That's the whole ambition of this area, and the first island of it is already alive in-engine.

The follow test

Our yardstick for "living," in one line: follow anyone — their day should make sense. The designed clock makes days real (a full day passes in about 24 minutes, and night acts through the world — eyes see less, some creatures wake — not through a screen filter). On top of it come schedules: a dockworker with a home, a shift, and a tavern he actually walks to. The schedule system is designed; the daily lives arrive with the crew milestone.

The three-year test

And the long version of that promise: follow one marine for three in-game years, and his life should add up. You'd see the morning drills — because training is real skill growth, not an animation. You might see the promotion — because his organization has real ranks to climb. Far from you, the world runs the same lives in fast, cheap strokes; near you, in full. The two must agree, and because the whole simulation is deterministic, that's a thing we can prove with tests rather than hope.

One seamless sea

The designed shape of the ocean itself: a single continuous sea, streamed in chunks, with no loading screens between you and the horizon. Distant ships don't vanish — they become ledger entries that keep sailing, and turn back into full ships as you close. In final design review now.

Islands worth the trip

World generation — islands, biomes, ports, the stuff between them — is charted, and it's deliberately late: engines first, then the content generator that fills them.

A world that runs without you is the only world worth rising in.
