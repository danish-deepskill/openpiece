Exploration

status: charted, the intent is set; it builds on the sailing that exists

The map starts blank, and filling it is the game. No fog-of-war checklist, no quest marker pointing at the horizon, just a sea that's bigger than what you know about it, and a hundred reasons to fix that.

Charts are items. They can be bought, sold, copied, stolen, and lied about. A good chart of dangerous waters is treasure in its own right.

Charting

What you discover goes on your chart: islands, ports, reefs, the strait that cuts a day off the southern run. Knowledge you hold in your hands, worth exactly what someone will pay, or steal.

Finding the way

Navigation is knowledge, not a minimap. The instruments, in the order you earn them:

dead reckoning the stars reading the wind a navigator's eye the Log Pose

A veteran navigator sees a route where a rookie sees open water; that gap is the navigator's whole job.

The sea never says no

Nothing here is fenced. The world gates you by what you know and what you can survive, never by an invisible wall.

A rookie in a rowboat can point the bow at the far seas today: the game won't stop you; the weather there and the navy there will. In the Grand Line the compasses spin, so you can only sail where a Log Pose points, and it learns islands one at a time: the frontier opens link by link. But everywhere you've been stays open behind you, and an Eternal Pose, a needle locked forever to one island, bought, looted, or stolen, lets you leap the chain entirely. Even the sea's last secret plays fair: it isn't hidden, it's defended, ringed by a storm no ship survives without what the longest hunt in the game earns. A wiki can tell you where it is. It can't sail you through.

Clues and rumors

Treasure hunting as real puzzling. The clue types the plan wants:

Fragments

Map pieces that only make sense combined, and the other halves are in other hands.

Riddles & songs

A verse with a bearing hidden in it, a carving that points at a landmark you have to recognize.

Rumors

A drunk sailor's story that's two-thirds wrong. The challenge isn't reaching the X, it's working out what the clue actually points to.

Where it stands

Charted. The sailing it stands on is built; the knowledge economy (charts, clues, rumors as things you hold and trade) is the design pass to come.

On this sea, knowledge is the treasure.
