Factions

status: built — living relations today; organizations as institutions charted

The sea belongs to powers older and bigger than you: the Marines, the pirate crews, the Revolutionaries, the kingdoms in between — and the wildlife that answers to nobody. They don't exist for your questline. They have their own relations, and you're just one more flag on their sea.

Relations, not teams

There are no "good guys" and "bad guys" flags in the engine — every faction holds a real relation to every other, and that data decides everything: who trades with you, who hunts you, who looks the other way. Wildlife is neutral until you give it a reason. Townsfolk defend themselves. Marines hunt pirates — including you, if that's the flag you fly. All of that runs in the game today.

Institutions, not spawn tables

The charted next step: organizations with an inside. A marine isn't "an enemy that spawns"; he's an ensign with a post, orders from a lieutenant, and a career. Follow him long enough and you might watch him earn his own command (see World for how far that promise goes).

The Marine hierarchy

The Marines are the flagship of the plan: a real chain of command from recruit to the seat at the top, where a rank is three concrete things — authority (what you may command: yourself, a squad, a ship, a base, a fleet), duties (patrol these lanes, garrison this base, inspect ships, escort convoys), and assets (the ship and garrison that rank actually holds). A Vice Admiral isn't a tougher enemy; he's a man who holds a branch base, with everything that means when you make him angry. Promotion is earned the hard way: deeds have to be seen and reported up the chain, a slot has to open, and politics gets its vote — the officer who survives your raid may climb because of you, and remember where his career came from.

Pirate crews have ranks too

A crew is an organization the same way — smaller, saltier, same bones. Captain, first mate, quartermaster; and the great crews of this sea run divisions under named commanders, fleets under one flag. Your own crew grows the same structure as it grows (see Crew) — and since every org is climbable, that ladder works with you on either end of it: hand out ranks aboard your ship, or earn one aboard somebody else's.

Your standing

Where you sit with each power is written by what you do and who saw you do it — that story has its own page: Bounty.

Factions remember. Plan accordingly.
