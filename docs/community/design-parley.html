Parley

status: charted, talking as real play, planned

Parley: the old pirate right to talk before anyone draws. In Openpiece, talking is playing, words are verbs with consequences, not a wall of text between you and the fight.

Some fights are won before they start: threaten a captain into striking his colors without a shot, if your name carries the weight to back it.

Words as verbs

Every one of these is a real move with a real cost, read against who you're talking to and what they know of you (see Bounty for how far your name reaches):

persuade bargain bribe bluff recruit threaten betray

Persuade the guard he didn't see you. Recruit the swordsman sulking in the tavern. Betray the partner who trusted you, once. The sea remembers which of these you reach for.

Information moves by mouth

Rumors, directions, warnings, lies: most of what you'll ever know about the sea arrives in conversation. The tavern is an instrument panel, if you know how to read one (see Exploration for what that knowledge is worth). And the talking isn't only for you: crews gossip among themselves, sailors trade news in every port, and your name travels through conversations you'll never hear.

Talk decides nothing, the world already has

A tavern keeper who knows a fact will tell it. A tavern keeper who doesn't, no line of dialogue will invent one.

Every word an NPC says is read out of what they actually know, feel, and have witnessed, the same ledgers that drive the rest of the sea. An optional voice can dress that up into richer prose, in your own words, running on your own machine, never our server and never a cost you pay us for, but it only ever describes the facts underneath. It cannot invent a route, grant a favor, or hand you something the world didn't decide. Say whatever you like to a guard: what happens next was never up to the words.

Say it your way, the game hears one thing

Type freely if you want, or just pick from what's plainly on offer, either way the game records one plain action underneath: you asked, you threatened, you lied. Talking your way past the rules works here exactly as well as talking your way past a locked door: persuasion is a real check against a real mind, not a trick of phrasing.

Every voice is a peer, so every voice gossips

The same law that makes every character a peer means an NPC can talk to another NPC exactly as you can. Two sailors trading a rumor in a port you've never set foot in still move something real, your name, your bounty, whatever they witnessed, and if you happen to be close enough to listen, you hear it exactly as they said it. Eavesdrop on the right conversation and you've found something no quest marker pointed you to.

Where it stands

The world already speaks in-engine, basic dialogue and interaction verbs run today. The full social game, persuasion, favors, standing, betrayal, is charted, and it grows alongside the crew and bounty systems it leans on.

Some doors only words open. Some fights are won before they start.
