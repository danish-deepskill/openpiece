Progression

status: charted, the laws are settled; the numbers await their pass

There is no XP bar. There are no levels. You become what you do, and the world keeps the receipts.

Growth here is a biography, not a bar filling up.

The three currencies

Everything you gain arrives through one of three doors, and none of them exchanges into another:

Capability

Skills grow by their own use. Sail to sail better, forge to smith better, survive fights to fight better. The doing itself is the teacher (see Activities).

Knowledge

Techniques, charts, counters, and secrets are learned from teachers, books, and paying attention, never "leveled." A veteran with identical stats beats a newbie (see Exploration).

Legend

Deeds that are witnessed become fame, and fame is social power: recruits seek you, doors open, enemies get worthier (see Bounty).

Your tier, the rank color over your head, derives from all three. It is a measurement of what you have become, never a thing you bought.

Tracks, not points

Nothing is purchased with generic points, because points earned anywhere and spent anywhere are just an XP bar wearing three costumes. Every track grows by its own exercise: endurance from exertion, toughness from taking hits, will from resisting fear, swordsmanship from swords. If a number moved, it moved because you did the thing.

You can't master everything

Skills cap, deliberately. The sea has master smiths who can't cook and navigators who lose fistfights, and you will be one of them: someone particular. Specialization is identity, and identity is what makes your crew need its other members (see Crew).

The shape of a career

The familiar arc is all here, but its boundaries are gradients (regions, skills, crew), never level gates. The world itself is the difficulty curve, and it gets wilder the further you sail.

  • Tutorial: there isn't one, on purpose. Your chosen start is your situation, the calm home seas are forgiving water, and learning to sail is, literally, learning to sail. The world does the teaching: your first stall dead into the wind, the first storm you should have reefed for, the first bar rumor that turned out to be true. Nothing pauses to explain itself, and nothing needs to.
  • Early game: one of the Blue Seas, the gentle outer waters. A nobody among fishing towns and small-time trouble: odd jobs and cargo runs, fish caught and meals cooked, dockside brawls with people as unimportant as you are. You learn the wind, pick your first fights, earn your first blade worth naming, and one day a marine writes your name down and the world officially knows you exist (see Story for the starts).
  • Mid game: the Grand Line's first half, Paradise (the name is the sea's own joke: it's only paradise compared to what follows), and the crew era. A real ship that needs real hands, and shipmates found one story at a time: the navigator you bailed out of a cell, the cook whose old crew left him ashore. Provisioning matters now, watches rotate, and your choices ripple: the poster with your face keeps a running count of everything you did to earn it. Organizations start paying attention, which is both the reward and the warning.
  • Late game: the New World, the Grand Line's far half, where the weather fights dirty and the routes belong to monsters. Command altitudes open: you stop winning fights alone and start winning them through people, squads under your orders, a rank or a territory under your flag, rivals whose careers the newspapers narrate against yours. The steel gets graded, the fruits get real, and the sea itself starts keeping score of your name.
  • End game: the Final New World, the last fifth of the sea, where only era-names sail, and the two summits it leads to. The seat of the world's power, reached by becoming undeniable: an emperor of territory, or the admiralty's peak, playing politics and wars whole regions feel. And the sea's last secret island, reached by knowing what no one alive knows complete: its route exists only as scattered pieces, in dead men's logs and enemies' vaults, and assembling it is the longest hunt in the game. One summit for strength, one for knowledge; the top of the world doesn't care which you climb.
  • Post game: there isn't one of these either, because the world doesn't end. Eras turn, rivals keep rising, and your legend becomes part of the world's history: a faded poster on a tavern wall, a name younger pirates swear by or swear at, an era someone new can be born into. And that someone can be you, starting again on the sea your last life shaped. And far beyond the known map, beyond any promise this page can make today, sailors whisper of the Dreadline: the sea's dark basement, where the dead still sail and madness is a compass. Sailors whisper a lot of things.

The world levels too

You are not the only one climbing. NPCs grow by the same three currencies, ambitious ones rise while you're not looking, and the marine you embarrassed in year one may be a commodore by year three (see the World page's three-year test). Standing still is falling behind, which is exactly what makes rivals worth having.

Fortune is real

Luck exists here, honestly. It acts on the world, never in the duel: a lucky sailor finds more washed ashore, docks on better days, and survives more interesting disasters. It offers opportunity, never outcomes, fortune hands you the story, and surviving it is still you. Low luck is never a curse, just an indifferent sea; high luck floods you with stories slightly bigger than you are. And sailors being sailors, a visibly lucky captain never wants for crew.

Choose your start

Instead of a story mode: origins. Dockside orphan, marine recruit, disgraced noble, freed captive, each a real opening situation with its own standing, place, and pull. The start shapes your circumstances; what you grow into is played, not scripted.

Where it stands

The laws above are settled direction; the formal design pass (curves, caps, the exact skill table) runs alongside the Powers content pass. The tier scale that measures it all is already live in-engine.

The sea keeps no XP bar. It keeps score in what you can do, what you know, and what they say about you.
